## Chapter 1 — ARRIVE

- **Voice/style decisions**: Maren's prose register is short declaratives, functional precision — she describes the city through what is wrong with it, not through how it makes her feel. Dry and exact. "Her coat past damp" rather than "she was soaked." "It had not resolved" about the corner building. The observational mode is her natural one.

- **Beat 1 register**: Half-page, single scene: Maren crossing a drainage channel, walking the last two blocks home. Rain present but never mentioned as weather — only its effects (fast channels, damp coat, wet street smell). Rats: three of them at the base of the east foundation wall. She watches them "not quite directly." They cross to the drain channel and go. She walks on. Unremarkable.

- **BEFORE timeline**: ~2–3 years before present. Ash lung is absent — Eli is healthy, unmentioned. This is established entirely through the atmosphere (ordinary, unhurried, no shadow on it) rather than any explicit statement.

- **Physical details established**:
  - Eli's lamp: always on his side of the table, so her side is in lower light. He arranged this without being asked. Stopped saying anything about it ~six years ago; just started doing it.
  - The table: he clears it when she comes home. She puts her bag in the space. Neither comments.
  - Her hands: open on the table, loose. She is not aware of this.

- **Eli's voice (BEFORE)**: Warm and precise without performing either quality. He reads student work aloud "not to her, exactly. Just: into the room." He does not ask for her opinion. When she brings him a problem — a word she needs — he works through it carefully, offers what he has, and says he'll keep looking. He does not ask her to thank him.

- **The student sentence** (read aloud by Eli in BEFORE): *"the machines inherited the sky and we were left with the names we used to have for it."* — established here. This sentence sets the standard every subsequent "reading aloud" moment will be measured against. Eli reads things into the room because he shares what catches him; future instances of this gesture must carry the same quality — precise, resonant, not on-the-nose. Do not use weaker material for it. Future chapters should not re-use this student's specific voice unless there is a deliberate reason.

- **The word that doesn't exist**: Maren asks for a word meaning "when an investigation has gone the right way but arrived at the wrong place." Eli does not find it. "Pyrrhic is close but it implies a victory." He offers: "the investigation found what it was looking for and the finding made things worse." He says it might be in a language that isn't this one. He says he'll keep looking. The word was never found. This exchange is the chapter's emotional center. It is also a seed: by Act Three, "found what it was looking for and made things worse" describes the arc of the story itself.

- **Structural landmarks**: None placed in this chapter (Beat 1 + BEFORE only — no structural phrase markers). "She did not sleep" does NOT appear here.

- **Separator between Beat 1 and BEFORE**: A plain paragraph break — no rule, no header, no date, no label. The temporal shift is unmarked. "She came in late" follows "She walked on" with only white space between them. The ambiguity is intentional: a reader may not know they've moved backward in time until Ch. 2 resets the present timeline. Do not insert a scene-break marker here in any future edit.

- **What future chapters must honor**:
  - The lamp arrangement. Whenever Maren and Eli share a table scene, his lamp is on his side.
  - The table clearance gesture. He does this. She does not comment.
  - Her hands open: this physical detail is meaningful and should recur in scenes where she is with him or thinking of him. Do not explain it.
  - The city's drainage channels: she knows the specific crossing point, two blocks from the apartment. She times them.
  - Eli's voice in BEFORE is distinct from his voice on the device (Act Two). Future sessions should honor the contrast: full sentences, texture, warmth — what is being lost is specifically this.
