What the Word Carries

She had written it in her notebook
without reaction.
A term. A container.
The kind of word you set down
and don't look at again.

Over four months
of handling what it described
it changed.

It holds the freeze —
the slow turn toward something
the room didn't contain for her,
the screen going dark,
the footsteps, small and fast,
into the baseboard,
into the east.

It holds *it needed following.*
The word from the old language
he had been reaching for
and never finished.

She could not give it back
in the condition she received it.
It had the corner slot in it,
the thirty-four minutes.

Below the window, a rat crossed the channel
without breaking stride.
Small. Purposeful.
Certain of something she could not perceive.

The drive was on the table.
She had not turned on the lamp.
Her hands were open in her lap.

She was leaning toward no.
It didn't feel like a decision.
It felt like a place she had already arrived
when she hadn't been looking.

The rain continued.
The dark shifted, barely, toward grey.
Not dawn. Just the city's version
of whatever comes before it.
