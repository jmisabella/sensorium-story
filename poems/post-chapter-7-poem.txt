From Inside

The lock engages before the door is tested.
She marks it — the only evidence
of a choice made
by someone who knew
what was coming down the corridor
and did not leave.

She does not yet know what to call this.

A gap in the baseboard.
The space between rooms three and four
where a woman moved
toward a stairwell
she did not reach.

The array gone, not broken —
taken by hands
that understood
what could not be repaired.

He had cleared the table
before he left.
Her side open.
The lamp on his side off.

She stands in the doorway
and finds it:
that this too was preparation,
the space made for her return,
that a room can be locked from inside
by someone who has already left —
the threshold held
by what he arranged before he went.

She does not turn on another light.
The channel outside runs grey.
Something moves along it and is gone.
