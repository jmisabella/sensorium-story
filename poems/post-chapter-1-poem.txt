What the Lamp Remembers

Rain runs the channels.
The city has been draining
for as long as anyone can name.

You come in late.
The table has been cleared.
The lamp is on his side.

He did not ask.
You did not say.
Six years ago the arrangement arrived
without announcement
and has not been revisited since.

He reads something into the room —
not to you, exactly.
Into the available air.
You don't look up.
That's good, you say.
He makes a note in the margin.

There is a word, you tell him,
for when an investigation
arrives correctly at the wrong place.
He says: it might exist
in a language that is not this one.
I'll keep looking.

You go back to the report.
The weight of it shifts.
Not lighter. Distributed.

At some point your hands are open on the table.
You cannot say when.

He moves through the dark apartment
with the ease of someone
who knows where everything is
and has stopped needing to check.

You sit in the remaining light.

Outside, the city goes on —
cycling, draining, persisting
in the way of something
that stopped noticing
it was tired.

You have not found the word.
You have not needed to.
