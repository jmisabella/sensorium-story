Adjustment

He put his hands on the table.
The old ink stain under his left thumb
had been there since before she knew him.

She had written the word in a report.
She had not slept the night after.

There had been a man on an observation table
who stopped mid-sentence
and turned his head toward something
she could not perceive —
something that called him
out of his words
and left them there, warm,
unfinished.

She had chosen the word.
Adjustment.

He said: the rest is substrate.

She heard him.
She believed he believed it.
She could not say the word.

The lamp threw its light toward him
and left her side lower,
the way he had arranged it years ago.
Rain ran in tracks along the glass,
branching, merging, continuing.

She was awake.
He was awake.
Neither said so.
The rain kept going.
