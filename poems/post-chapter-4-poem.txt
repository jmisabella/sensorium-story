The Interface

She wrote it down the way you write
what you expect to use —
exact words, exact sequence,
the pause before *merely*
marked the way she marks a hesitation
in a witness.

*The pattern adapts;
the sensorium is merely the interface.*

Outside, the rain.
The channel carrying color
from somewhere upstream.

She has written things like this before
in other rooms
and found them exact
and put them away.

She stands at the rail
and finds that the line
has different weight
in rain
than it had under strip lighting.

The instrument and the music.
The pattern and the substrate.
The man she could describe
without mentioning
what he reads into available air,
what he notes in margins,
the ease with which he moves
through the dark
knowing where everything is.

*That* is not what the word means.

She crosses the bridge.
She carries the sentence east
in the sequence she wrote it,
and finds that a thing
can be precisely true
and cost something anyway.
