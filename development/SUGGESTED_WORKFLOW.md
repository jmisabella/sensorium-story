
# Practical Workflow

The Eli degradation arc is the hardest thing to control. It's a gradual, precise erosion — full sentences → pauses → shorter sentences → five words → gone. If you revise Chapter 6 before writing Chapter 14, you don't yet know whether the pacing of the degradation actually landed. You'll polish a scene that may need restructuring once you can see the full arc. Better to draft it rough, read the whole thing, and then adjust the pacing of the decline with full visibility.

The word "sensorium" needs to be managed across the whole manuscript. Three appearances, each costing more than the last. This is impossible to calibrate from inside a single chapter session. It's a revision-pass task, not a drafting task.

Early chapters will need to change once you know the ending. They always do. Revising Chapter 2 to a finished state before Chapter 20 exists is wasted work.

# Practical Workflow
Phase 1 — Scene Sketches (current phase)
7 key scenes drafted as working prose. This is already underway. These anchor the story's voice and emotional register before anything else.

## Phase 2 — Rough Draft
One chapter (or two short ones) per session. The goal for each session is: complete the beats, move forward, do not stop to polish. A handoff note at the end of each session — a few lines summarizing where the chapter left off and what the next one needs to establish — will orient the next session without requiring the whole manuscript to be re-read.

The development docs (especially PLOT_OUTLINE.md) are the constant reference. Each session reads the relevant beats before writing.

## Phase 3 — Read the whole draft before touching anything
This is the step most people skip and it's the most important one. Read it start to finish with fresh eyes. Note what's working and what isn't. Do not edit during this read — just see it.

## Phase 4 — Revision passes (thematic, not chapter-by-chapter)

- Pass 1 Focus:	Arc and structure — do the three acts land? Are the beats earning their weight?
- Pass 2 Focus: Eli's degradation arc — is the pacing of the decline consistent and earned?
- Pass 3 Focus: "Sensorium" tracking — every appearance, does the meaning shift correctly?
- Pass 4 Focus: Maren's voice — consistent? Does her doubt feel accumulated, not sudden?
- Pass 5 Focus: Prose and line level — this is the last pass, not the first

## The Corner We're Trying Not to Paint Into

The one real risk with this story: the investigation plot and the grief plot run in parallel and need to converge at the right moment (Maren finds the archive just as Eli's decline is hitting its worst). If one thread moves faster than the other, the structure breaks.

The way to protect against this: the PLOT_OUTLINE.md beats are your pacing contract. As long as each chapter is hitting its designated beat and not drifting into territory that belongs to a later beat, the two threads will converge where they're supposed to. Trust the outline during the rough draft; interrogate the pacing during Revision Pass 1.
