# Sensorium — Chapter Writing Prompts

Each session below is a self-contained prompt for a fresh Claude Code session to write one chapter of the novella. Copy and paste the relevant session's full prompt to begin.

Completed chapters are saved to `chapters/` at the project root. Create this directory if it does not yet exist.

**"She did not sleep" refrain — GLOBAL RULE**: This phrase appears in several sketches but must appear in full prose **no more than 3 times total**: Chapter 8 (First Contact), Chapter 13 (The Freeze), and Chapter 20 (The Choice). All other sleeplessness requires different language.

**"Sensorium" word — GLOBAL RULE**: Appears exactly 3 times in prose: Chapter 4 (Harlan's briefing — clinical, costs nothing), Chapter 13 aftermath (costs something), Chapter 20 (costs everything). Never in the Epilogue.

## RISK WARNING

The one real risk with this story: the investigation plot and the grief plot run in parallel and need to converge at the right moment (Maren finds the archive just as Eli's decline is hitting its worst). If one thread moves faster than the other, the structure breaks.

**Write Chapter 1 — ARRIVE.**

This chapter does two things without any break between them: it opens on Maren walking home through the city (Beat 1), and immediately — no chapter break, no date, no header — flows into the BEFORE sketch (an ordinary evening 2–3 years prior). The reader arrives at the apartment having already seen what she walks home through.

**Beat 1 (half-page — city opener):**
- Rain. Night. Maren walking home. We do not yet know where from.
- The city: wet concrete, drainage channels running fast, smell of damp stone and rot at the margins, buildings tending toward collapse. Lights on in some windows.
- Rats move along the base of the walls. She watches them the way someone watches something they have learned not to look at directly.
- Establish Maren's voice: observational, contained, dry-precise.
- The rats are present and unremarkable — not yet charged with meaning.

**Immediately following (BEFORE sketch — under 600 words, possibly much shorter):**
- A pre-story evening: Eli and Maren, ordinary, before ash lung was a daily presence.
- Nothing dramatic happens. Two people being exactly themselves with each other.
- Eli has cleared the table. He always does. She puts her bag in the space.
- He reads a line aloud from something he's been marking — not for her, just because he shares things when they catch him. She doesn't look up. She hears it.
- He finds the word for something she came in carrying. Offers it without ceremony.
- Her hands are open in her lap. She is not aware of this.
- Do NOT foreshadow ash lung or the transplant. Do NOT editorialize the tenderness. Do NOT have them declare their love.
- The restraint is structural. This scene works by being ordinary.

**Act break note:** A blank page with the Roman numeral **I** precedes this chapter in the manuscript.

**Write Chapter 6 — THE WEIGHT.**

**Beat 7 (The Conversation They Haven't Had):**
- Eli and Maren in the apartment. He has had a medical assessment; the progression is clearer now. He raises the transplant — not for the first time, but differently this time.
- He is not emotional. He has been thinking. He says: *the mind is what I am. The transplant preserves the mind. I've read what the early transplants say.*
- She cannot say what is actually wrong — cannot describe the freeze she witnessed three years ago, cannot explain why "adjustment" is not a good enough word — because saying it requires her to believe it, and she has been refusing to believe it for three years.
- She argues: the program, the timing, the uncertainty. He listens carefully. He does not agree.
- Neither resolves it. They go to sleep in the same bed, not touching, both awake.

**REQUIRED — Health suspicion seed:**
Somewhere in this scene — briefly, without announcement — Maren registers something she does not say. A cough she suppresses before it starts, or a breath she notices on the stairs coming home. She has been doing this for eight months. She does not say it to him. He may notice. He does not ask. The reader should feel, in retrospect, that this moment was always here.

**Craft note:** This is not a scene about two people failing to connect. It is a scene about two people whose connection is strong enough to hold the weight of what neither is saying. The not-touching is not distance — it is each of them trying not to transfer what they're carrying to the other. The love is present in the restraint.

**Write Chapter 7 — THE PROCEDURE.**

This chapter carries three beats and ends Act One. It should feel like the point of no return — because it is.

**Beat 8 (Inciting Incident):**
- A major act of sabotage: a large transplant facility in the eastern district. Equipment destroyed in a manner requiring planning and inside knowledge. Several people killed — not Institute staff but transfer candidates in the pre-procedure wing. People who were *waiting*.
- The calculus has changed. The Council is demanding results.
- Maren and Soren are at the scene. She does her job. She is very good at it. She finds three things: a supply chain signature she's seen before; an unusual access pattern; a small detail that doesn't fit — she pockets it mentally, unresolved.

**Beat 9 (Eli's Decision):**
- He tells her in the apartment, quietly. He does not ask her permission; he asks her to hear him.
- Key line (keep near-verbatim): *"I know what it costs. I'd rather pay it."*
- She tries to answer this. She fails. She cannot describe the freeze. She cannot say the specific thing. The appointment is made.

**Beat 10 (The Transplant Day):**
- Maren does not attend the ceremony. She has not attended one in three years. She works.
- Late afternoon: her device signals. The procedure is complete. The body has been cremated. The rat has woken.
- She reads the message twice. She puts the device in her pocket. She finishes the report she was writing. Outside the window, it is raining.

**Craft note — required in Beat 10:** Two or three sentences should carry: she was not at the ceremony, and he would have expected her not to be. Not because he doesn't want her there. Because seven years have taught him the shape of what she can and cannot do, and he did not ask her to be different. That he already knew this about her — that he made room for exactly who she is, even at this — is its own form of love. And its own form of grief.

**END OF ACT ONE.**

**Write Chapter 13 — THE FREEZE.**

This is the most important chapter in the story. Handle it accordingly.

**Beats 17 (Network's Edge) + Soren appearance #2 + 18 (THE FREEZE):**

**Beat 17 — Investigation reaches its edge:**
- By this beat, Maren has enough to close the case — enough to hand to Soren, enough to identify the network's leadership. She chooses not to.
- She has a location. She does not log it. She has made the decision before she opens the door.

**SOREN ACT TWO APPEARANCE #2:**
- He asks about a lead she hasn't shared, or notes a gap in her log. She gives him just enough. A partial truth, a redirect. He lets it go.
- Neither of them says what they both understand. This plants the seed for Chapter 17.

**Beat 18 — THE FREEZE (the story's most important scene):**
- Mid-conversation with Eli. He is in the middle of a sentence — something precise, something she has been leaning into — when he goes still.
- Not absent. *Oriented.* His body shifts — a fraction, then more — toward the far corner of the room. Not agitated. Focused. Completely, entirely focused on something the room does not appear to contain for her.
- The screen goes dark.
- She says his name.
- Nothing.
- She waits. She is very still. She waits for longer than feels reasonable. She says his name again.
- Footsteps. Small, fast. He has left the room. Through the rat-access corridor. Wherever he has gone, he has taken it with him.
- She sits in the empty room for a long time.
- The screen activates, eventually: *sorry. came back. something — I don't have the word. a pressure. it needed following.*
- She writes: *subject demonstrated expected sensory response.* She is the only one who will read this report. She goes home.

**STRUCTURAL LANDMARK — "She did not sleep" #2:** This phrase appears here. One of only three permitted appearances.

**STRUCTURAL LANDMARK — "Sensorium" appearance #2 (REQUIRED):**
In the aftermath — in her internal processing, not in the report — the word surfaces. She reaches for a way to describe what she just watched, and finds Harlan's vocabulary waiting: *the sensorium*. She is using the clinical term of the man who buried the cure to name what just took Eli out of the room mid-sentence. She notices she is doing this. The word belongs to him and she cannot escape it. **This is the first time "sensorium" costs something.**

- Pure sensation and behavior. No character interiority. No identification. No confirmation.
