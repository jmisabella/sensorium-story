### ISSUES TO ADDRESS

Known remaining issues to address (in priority order):

1. Repetitive short / single-sentence / locomotion chapter endings
   - Very frequent endings like “She walked home through the rain.” “She went out.” “She did not sleep.” “She walked.” “She stood there for a moment.” “She left.” etc.
   - Appears in ~70% of chapters → feels mechanical, reduces emotional carry-over.

2. Rain / drainage / mineral description clusters
   - Overused in Ch. 1–3, 6, 8–15, 18–20. Some feel near-redundant (“rain fell steadily / the rain was the same / drainage running fast / mineral staining”).

3. Dialogue tag overuse
   - Clusters of “she said” / “he said” / “said Eli” in dialogue-heavy scenes (Ch. 8, 10, 12, 13, 15, 19 especially).

4. Minor rhythm flattening & overwriting
   - Dense clusters of short declaratives that flatten mood.
   - Occasional slight overwriting in descriptive passages (rain, open hands, “the rain was the same” variants).

5. Open-hands motif check
   - Appears 7–8 times. Confirm all are earned; flag any redundancy (esp. Ch. 2 and Ch. 6).

Task (perform in this exact order):

A. Chapter Endings Fix — highest priority
   1. List EVERY chapter-final sentence or short paragraph that is:
      - Single short sentence
      - Ends on locomotion (“walked”, “went”, “left”, “stood”, “sat”, “moved”)

      - Or feels abrupt/clipped
   2. For EACH one, provide ONE improved replacement (1–3 sentences max) that:
      - Echoes an existing motif (rain/drainage, open hands, missing word, sensorium shift, rat movement, mineral memory, weight redistribution)
