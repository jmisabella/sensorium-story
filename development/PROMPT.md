**Draft Scene Sketch #1: THE FREEZE (Beat 18 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

This is the most important scene in the story — the moment mid-conversation when Eli-rat goes still, orients toward something Maren cannot perceive, and leaves mid-sentence. The human mind losing to the rat sensorium in real time. It should be the most disturbing scene in the entire three-novella collection.

Relevant details:
- POV is Maren's, external only. We never enter the rat's mind. The horror is what she observes.
- Eli communicates through the neural readout device — text on a screen at floor level.
- The room is a rat-accessible space in their building. She is sitting. He is somewhere in the room.
- He goes still mid-sentence — not absent, but *oriented*. Focused. Completely.
- The screen goes dark. She says his name. Nothing. He leaves through a rat-access corridor.
- She waits a long time. Eventually the screen returns with a brief message: something like *sorry. came back. something — I don't have the word. a pressure. it needed following.*
- She writes in her report: "subject demonstrated expected sensory response." She goes home. She doesn't sleep.
- Atmosphere: almost certainly raining. The room is dim, small, functional. The terminal at floor level.
- Eli's voice through the device should still be recognizably HIM — precise, careful — which is exactly what makes it devastating when he leaves.
- This scene occurs in Act Two, roughly months 3-4 post-transfer. The pauses have already started. This is the first full disappearance.

**Draft Scene Sketch #2: FIRST CONTACT (Beat 11 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

Maren's first conversation with Eli-rat through the device, the day after the transplant. Recognition horror: he is there, fully, and also not there at all.

Relevant details:
- This is the post-transfer recovery room at the Institute — a small space designed for the transition period, rat-accessible, communication terminal at floor level.
- This is the day after. The body has been cremated. She has not slept.
- His first message contains something she recognizes immediately — a specific word, a phrase, something exactly his. This is the moment of recognition. Relief and horror arriving together.
- He describes the sensory environment: the scale of the room (enormous to him now), the smell of her (complex, specific, she has a smell she has never thought about before), the whiskers as spatial mapping. He is trying to translate something that has no human frame of reference, and he is doing it with characteristic precision.
- He is entirely Eli. She can hear him. And he is in a rat's body, describing a rat's world, which she cannot follow him into.
- POV is Maren's only. We observe Eli through the device screen and through her attention.
- Tone: not yet grief — something earlier and more disorienting. The gap hasn't opened yet. It's opening now, for the first time.
- This scene is the counterweight to the freeze: he is fully present here. By the freeze, he will be half-gone. The contrast is the arc.
- This scene occurs the day after the Act One ending (Eli's transplant).

**Draft Scene Sketch #3: HARLAN'S SENSORIUM SPEECH (Beat 4 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

Harlan delivers his key line in a Council-level security briefing or Institute lecture. The context in which it sounds clinical and reasonable. Maren is present. She writes it in her notes.

Relevant details:
- The locked key line, keep near-verbatim: *"The sensorium is malleable. We are not bound to one set of inputs. The pattern adapts; the sensorium is merely the interface."*
- Context: he is answering a question — probably from a Council member or skeptic — about whether the transplanted are really still *themselves*. His answer is a reasonable response to that question, in a briefing room.
- Harlan's manner: precise, measured, without warmth but not unkind. He does not perform certainty — he simply has it. He is patient. He has answered this question before and he will answer it again.
- Maren writes the line in her notes. It costs her nothing yet. She tells herself this.
- The scene should establish Harlan as someone whose authority is earned, whose argument is internally coherent, and who is not a caricature. He is the most dangerous kind of wrong: partly right.
- Atmosphere: institutional. The briefing room or lecture hall is functional, dim, the kind of space that has been used for decades without being renovated. This is the city's governing apparatus in material form.
- This is Act One, before Eli's transplant. Maren is still formally a program defender.
- The scene establishes the first appearance of the word "sensorium" in the story — clinical, dismissive, reasonable. Track this; the word's meaning will change.

**Draft Scene Sketch #4: DISCOVERY OF THE ARCHIVE (Beat 21 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

Maren accesses Harlan's restricted archive and finds Nadia's buried research — the suppressed trail toward a cure. The moment she understands what she is looking at.

Relevant details:
- This follows Voss (a junior Institute data researcher) pointing Maren toward the archive's location — Voss does this honestly, without understanding what she's pointed Maren toward.
- What Maren finds: Nadia's preliminary CDFS research files. Methodology notes, early results, a summary pointing toward a molecular inhibitor pathway that might arrest fibrosis progression in early-stage patients. Not a finished cure — a trail. The data is real and it is good.
- At the bottom of the file record: Harlan's administrative note. *REVIEWED — REASSIGNED — ARCHIVE.* Twelve years ago.
- The scene is the moment of understanding: this was not lost. This was put away.
- Maren's reaction is not rage — it is something colder and more complicated. She is an investigator; this is what investigation feels like when you find the thing. But this specific thing has a weight that an ordinary discovery does not.
- She reads it twice. She sits with it. She thinks about Eli. She thinks about the freeze. She does not know yet whether the research would have worked. She does know it was buried.
- The scene also contains the first connection to Nadia — at this point in the story, Maren has already been building toward Nadia through the investigation. The data and the person are about to converge.
- Atmosphere: wherever she accesses the archive — an Institute terminal, a data room, possibly late at night. The city outside, raining.

**Draft Scene Sketch #5: THE CONFRONTATION WITH HARLAN (Beat 23 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

Maren goes to Harlan with the archive data. He does not deny it. He explains. Some of what he says is not wrong. That is the horror.

Relevant details:
- Maren comes alone. She has not told Soren. She has not decided what she will do with the data yet — she needs to hear him first.
- Harlan does not deny the suppression. He explains the pragmatic calculation: the research needed 5+ years of testing; during that time voluntary transplant participation would have collapsed; people would die waiting for something that might never materialize; if the research failed, the program would have lost irreplaceable momentum.
- He also gives the ideological argument: the transplant is not just a survival measure — it is the correct direction for humanity regardless of the cure question. The cure, if it worked, would be a temporary fix for biological fragility in a world that is becoming less habitable. He did not suppress hope. He chose the better hope.
- He addresses the drift: he calls it adaptation. The pattern persists; it operates through a new interface; human-coded behavior is not the measure of continuity. *"The sensorium is merely the interface"* — he may echo this line here in defense of the drift, which lands very differently now than it did in the briefing room.
- He ends: *"I did not make this decision carelessly. I made it carefully. That is different from making it wrongly."*
- Maren cannot dismiss everything he says. The window of uncertainty in the research is real. The cost of false hope is real. This is what makes the scene have teeth — she came in with certainty and leaves without it.
- His manner throughout: calm, patient, not defensive. He has lived inside this argument for twelve years. Every objection has been absorbed.
- The scene must not resolve. Maren leaves without telling him what she will do.

Save the completed sketch to `development/sketches/sketch_05_confrontation.md`.

**Draft Scene Sketch #6: THE SUSPENDED CHOICE (Beat 26 in PLOT_OUTLINE.md).**

The final chapter. Rain. Maren alone with the archive data and the impossible choice. She leans toward not going through with it. The story ends without resolving.

Relevant details:
- This is the story's last chapter before the epilogue. It should be short — not a summary, not an explanation. A moment held.
- The archive data is in her hands (or on her device). She could: release it publicly and force an inquiry; pursue the inhibitor pathway independently; or do what Eli did.
- The cure, even if it existed, only works pre-transplant. This changes nothing for Eli. He stays in the rat regardless of what she does. She knows this.
- What the data means *for her*: if the research trail is valid, she might not have to do what Eli did. The choice about the data is structurally also a choice about her own body.
- She has heard Harlan. She cannot dismiss him.
- She has heard Eli: *"I don't want you to become this."* Five words, said with what he had left.
- She is leaning toward not going through with the transplant — but the story does not confirm this. It suspends at the moment of leaning.
- Outside the window: rain. The city as it always is. A rat crosses a drainage channel without breaking stride.
- The word "sensorium" should appear here for the third time — or be notably, deliberately absent. It has become an open wound. Handle it carefully.
- No resolution. No release. The story ends while she is still there.

**Draft Scene Sketch #7: THE EPILOGUE.**

Two rats. No names. No interior. No confirmation. The reader decides.

Relevant details:
- Pure sensory and behavioral description only. No character interiority. No identification. No confirmation of who these rats are or are not.
- Setting: a drainage channel beneath the eastern district at night. Real infrastructure — maintenance tunnels, running water, the smell of concrete and mineral seep and whatever the city has drained into this system for two hundred years.
- The prose should work entirely through sensation: what the water smells like (complex, layered, carrying information a human nose cannot decode), the texture of the concrete under their feet (every irregularity registered through vibration before sight), the sound map of the underground (distant machinery, water two levels below, something electrical in the walls).
- The two rats move together without apparent coordination — not mirroring, but in rhythm. One pauses; the other waits without being asked to.
- The rain, above: audible as a shift in pressure, a change in the air coming through the street grates. One rat pauses. Orients upward — toward the sound and smell of rain. The posture is held, attentive, longer than instinct would strictly require.
- Then they continue, together, into the dark.
- This section should be under 1,000 words. Possibly much shorter. It does not explain. It does not resolve. It simply is.
- The ambiguity must be complete: a reader who believes it is Maren and Eli should find evidence for that reading. A reader who believes it is two ordinary rats should find evidence for that reading. Neither should be able to convince the other.
- The body horror details established earlier (the sensory overwhelm, the scent world, the rat's relationship to its environment) pay off here — but rendered as beauty, not horror. The epilogue should feel different from the body horror of Act Two. These two rats are at home.

Save the completed sketch to `development/sketches/sketch_07_epilogue.md`.
