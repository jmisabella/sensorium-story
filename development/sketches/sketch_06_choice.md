# Sketch 06 — The Suspended Choice
*Beat 26 | Act Three — Final Chapter*
*Working draft — not final prose*

---

The archive data was on her device. She had been holding it for twenty minutes without opening it.

The window was wet — steady rain, the drainage channel below running dark and fast. She had watched two cycles of it, and then started counting something she couldn't name the unit of, and then stopped. The room was the same room it had always been.

She opened the device. She looked at the files.

---

What they meant for Eli: nothing.

The cure — if it was a cure, if the pathway led somewhere, if twelve years of suppressed preliminary data pointed toward anything real — worked pre-transplant. Harlan had said this. The literature said the same thing. You could not undo the transfer. The pattern was written. Whatever lived in the rat was what remained, and the remainder was permanent, and the research in her hands changed exactly nothing about that.

He was in the infrastructure somewhere — the eastern district, most likely, with the others. She thought about Dael's family: *he seems okay. He just prefers it there now.*

She knew what *prefers* meant. She had known for a long time.

---

What the data meant for her.

She made herself think about this directly. She had been not-thinking about it for three days, since the archive room, and she had been not-thinking about the cough for eight months before that. The same category of not-looking, and she was done with it tonight.

She had a cough. She had been having a cough since last spring. She was breathless on stairs she had climbed for seven years without noticing them. She had not been tested. She had not wanted to know what she was looking at, and she recognized what that was.

If the research led somewhere — if the inhibitor pathway was what the early data suggested — then she might not have to do what Eli did.

She held this. She let it be what it was.

---

Harlan's voice, ordered and exact: *between eight and fifteen percent of promising early-stage work produces clinically viable results under ideal conditions. The conditions were not ideal.*

She had not been able to find where it didn't hold. She had tried. Four days of trying, and the calculation was real, the cost of false hope was something she had watched people absorb in her work, people who had waited for things that did not materialize and died in the waiting. He had buried research that might not have led anywhere, in order to preserve a path that worked. She could not know whether the people who were transferred would have survived a treatment timeline. She could not know whether the treatment would have worked. The window of uncertainty was exactly the size he'd said it was.

*I did not make this decision carelessly. I made it carefully. That is different from making it wrongly.*

He believed this. She had been able to tell that he believed it. That was the part she had not prepared for.

---

And Eli's voice: five words, said with what he had left.

*I don't want you to become this.*

She had put her hand near the terminal. He had oriented toward the warmth of it.

---

She tried to answer Nadia's question.

She had been walking around it since the first time Nadia asked it — not refusing, exactly, but navigating the edges, mapping the shape without looking at the center. Tonight she looked.

*Is that Eli in the rat, or a pattern that believes it is Eli?*

She thought about the first morning. The screen activating — *you're here. I could hear you in the corridor* — and beneath that, the specific construction: *I am trying to find the words in the right order.* The teacher's exactness. The sense that language had a proper sequence and you owed it to the words to find it. She had recognized it before she'd processed it, recognized it the way you recognized a voice in a room before you'd turned to look. That was him. She had been certain.

She thought about the freeze. He had been mid-sentence — something about thought arriving differently now, the spacing of it, the room it left — and then he was gone. Not absent. Oriented. His body shifting toward the corner, the slow rotation completing itself, his attention directed at something the room did not contain for her. The screen going dark. His name in the empty room, once and then again. Footsteps, small and fast, into the slot in the baseboard.

*I don't have the word. a pressure. it needed following.*

He had always had the word. Finding the word was the shape of who he was — how he worked, how he loved, the most specific thing about him, the thing she had relied on for seven years and not named until she was watching it go. And the rat had pulled him out of a sentence before he could finish it. Had followed something she could not perceive through a gap in the wall while the terminal went dark and she sat counting drips.

She tried to hold both things at the same time. The voice she had recognized on the first morning, completely and immediately. The body that had left mid-sentence without looking back.

She tried to say whether they were the same thing.

She could not.

The question did not resolve. She pressed on it and it did not give. Maybe from a different position it would resolve — from inside the rat, maybe, or from some vantage point that could see both mornings at once. She did not have that position. She stayed with the not-resolving and tried to let it be the shape of the thing rather than a failure to see the shape.

---

When she tried to describe what had happened — what had happened in the freeze, in the shortening sentences, in the four months of watching the edges of him recede — she kept arriving at the word Harlan had used. The sensorium. She had written it in her notebook in the briefing room and told herself it cost her nothing. It had seemed reasonable then. A technical term: the sum of perceptual inputs, the interface through which a consciousness received the world.

She could not use it neutrally now. The word had the freeze in it. The footsteps, small and fast. The slot in the baseboard. *It needed following.* The rat sensorium pulling him out of language, out of the room, out of the sentence he was trying to finish for her. The interface had won. The word belonged to that now.

---

Below the window, a rat crossed the drainage channel without breaking stride. Small, purposeful, moving through the water in the dark with a certainty she could not account for.

She watched it until it was out of sight.

---

She was leaning toward no.

She had known this for some time. She had been staying in the question because the question felt more honest than the answer — and because she had owed herself the full weight of it first. Harlan's argument, Nadia's question, Eli's five words, her own cough she hadn't tested. She had stayed with all of it. She had tried to answer what she could.

The research might lead somewhere. That was real. She was not setting it aside. She was letting it be part of the weight, not a resolution and not a thing she could dismiss. If the inhibitor pathway was what the early data suggested, there might come a time when the choice she was facing now was not the only choice. She did not know this. She was allowing herself to not-know it while leaning, still, toward no.

This did not feel like a decision. It felt like something that had already happened, completed in some earlier moment she had not noticed, and she was only now looking at the place where it had occurred.

She did not know whether she was right. She had stopped expecting to know.

---

She was still at the window when the light changed. The dark outside shifting, barely, toward the grey that preceded morning in this city — not true dawn, not yet, the rain thinning into a different register, something almost quiet.

She had not slept.

She did not close the device. She sat in the remaining dark with her hands open in her lap and watched the city do what the city always did: drain, persist, move water from one level to the next, carry whatever the rain brought with it out toward the eastern district and the channels below.

She was still there.
