# Sketch 07 — The Epilogue
*Post-story | Beneath the eastern district*
*Working draft — not final prose*

---

The water carries the city's information downward.

The channel runs below the eastern district at the third maintenance level — concrete old enough to have absorbed decades of mineral seep, the walls presenting a record that extends deeper than any single crossing. Iron from the broken filtration loop three junctions west. Organic matter in slow decomposition. Ammonia traces, faint, the mark of creatures that had been here hours before. The particular mineral signature of rain-diluted runoff from the streets above, which smells different from still water, different from piped water, different again from the standing pools that collect in the lower levels in dry periods — though there were no dry periods.

Beneath the eastern district, at night, the channel runs.

Two rats move along the western ledge.

Their route is established — not by agreement, not by following, but by accumulated use: the ledge worn at specific places, the corners navigated at specific speeds. One moves slightly ahead. The other follows the same line, the same spacing from the wall, without instruction.

The floor registers everything. Every crack in the concrete, every irregularity, every variation in surface moisture. The weight-shift over a loose aggregate piece. The gradient change where the ledge meets the junction ahead, which the vibration of footsteps reports before the eyes have turned. The body knows the channel's geometry in its feet.

The air above the water is different from the air against the wall. The water carries city information — everything the drainage system has touched in the last six hours, flowing south from the residential sectors, translated by the channel into a record that can be read. The smell of a kitchen, diluted, carried seven blocks. Industrial lubricant from the maintenance depot. Soil from the small courtyard gardens that still operated in the fourth sector, their particular mineral signature recognizable, distinct from the concrete and iron that made up everything else.

It is not overwhelming. It is rich. It is the city as the city is, without abbreviation.

Distant: machinery at some regular interval. Pumps, or the deep infrastructure of the water system, something electric and rhythmic that sends a vibration through the lowest level of the walls, barely audible, felt rather than heard. Further below, water moving fast through a narrower channel — its sound arriving through stone as a pressure rather than a noise, a presence rather than a sound.

The two rats continue.

At the junction where the eastern channel meets the overflow branch, one pauses.

The other stops. Waits.

Not because of a signal. Simply: one stopped, and the other did not continue. They remain this way — a few lengths apart, in the dark, in the smell of iron and mineral and the long record of the city. No contact. No visible communication. One paused; the other is there.

Then — from the grates above: a shift.

The air changes before the sound arrives. A pressure difference, slight, coming down through the gaps in the street-level gratings — a new quality of smell, a new quality of sound. Rain. The upper city doing what the upper city always did, water beginning again on the streets above, finding the drainage lines, starting its descent toward here.

One rat orients upward.

The posture holds. Head angled toward the nearest grate, nose lifted into the changed air, body still except for the whiskers, which move slowly through what the rain is bringing down — reading the arrival the way you read something you have been waiting for without knowing you were waiting.

The rain, above: complex, layered, carrying the smell of the sky — what the clouds had collected, what they had moved through, the particular quality of water that had been suspended and is now released. Not like the channel water. Not like the stored water. The rain smells like distance.

The posture holds. A count of heartbeats. Then more.

The second rat has not moved.

Then the first rat lowers its head. The whiskers settle. It turns, and continues along the ledge.

The second follows — the same spacing, the same line.

Together they move into the further dark of the channel, where the city's drainage carries the rain down and down, where the infrastructure that has been moving water for two hundred years continues its work without recognition, without rest.

The sound of them diminishes.

Then is gone.

The channel remains: the water, the iron smell, the distant pulse of pumps, the rain arriving steadily through the grates above and finding, as rain always found, the lowest place.
