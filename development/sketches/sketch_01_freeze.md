# Sketch 01 — The Freeze
*Beat 18 | Act Two, months 3–4 post-transfer*
*Working draft — not final prose*

---

The rain had been steady since before she arrived. She could hear it through a gap in the ceiling panel — a seam where the original room and the rat-accessible addition met unevenly, water finding the line, the way water always found the line. Three years ago the building's board had called it a temporary adjustment. She had been here seven times.

The room was small. She had brought her own chair, the first time; now there was a chair here, kept here, slightly too large for the space. The terminal sat at floor level along the far wall: a screen the width of her palm, mounted in a bracket someone had installed correctly, the surface still clear compared to everything else in the building. She had learned to sit forward, elbows on her knees, to see the screen without bending.

Eli was speaking.

The device translated his signal to text one phrase at a time, with the rhythm he had developed — not punctuated the way his speech had been, more like a series of careful arrivals. He had been describing what he noticed about his own thinking. How the intervals had changed.

*it takes longer now. and shorter. both. a thought arrives and there is space around it I didn't have before. like a room with the furniture moved out. which sounds frightening but it doesn't feel frightening it feels—*

A pause. Normal — he paused. She had been learning to wait them out. Not to ask *are you there* the way she'd wanted to in the first weeks.

*I had a word for it. a good word. I'll come back to—*

He stopped.

Not a pause. Something different.

He was at the far end of the room, near the corner where the baseboard met the east wall. His body had shifted — a fraction, then more, a slow rotation she watched complete itself without understanding it. His head angled toward the wall. His sides stopped moving at the rate they had been moving.

She waited.

The screen held the partial sentence: *I'll come back to—*

She waited.

The screen went dark.

She said his name.

Nothing. The room. The rain.

She stayed seated. This was a decision she made without making it — her body choosing not to stand, not to move toward him, not to do anything that would change what was already happening. She was very still. She counted the drips from the ceiling panel. Three seconds. Four. Irregular. The way drips were.

"Eli."

Quietly. Not a call. He was there, in the corner, fully there, his attention aimed at something that occupied a frequency she could not reach. His stillness was not the stillness of absence. It was the opposite: complete focus, directed entirely away from her.

Then the footsteps. Small and fast, a sound she had learned to separate from the building's other sounds — barely audible, already receding. Past the terminal. Along the baseboard. Into the slot at the far left corner, a gap in the trim darker than the wall around it.

Gone.

---

She sat in the empty room.

She did not know how long. The rain. The three-second drip. The terminal screen, dark, waiting for a signal that was not coming from anywhere near this room.

At some point she stopped watching the corner slot and looked at the terminal instead. As if looking were a kind of waiting that would accelerate waiting.

It was not.

She said his name again. Once. Into the empty room. Not because she expected it to do anything.

She had her report open on her device. She looked at the last line she'd written, from thirty minutes ago, and did not add to it.

---

Eventually: small claws on concrete. Faint, then faint, then nothing, then the creak of the slot. A pause. Then the terminal screen activated.

*sorry. came back. something — I don't have the word. a pressure. it needed following.*

She read it three times.

She had been there when he explained the whiskers — the way they mapped air displacement, registered the room before his eyes had turned toward it, gave him a spatial sense that worked at a different speed than sight. He had found the right words for that. He always found the right words. The first weeks, she had almost been able to follow him.

*I don't have the word.*

She looked at the incomplete sentence still showing in the terminal's buffer: *I'll come back to—*

He had been talking about language. How thought moved differently now, arrived at different intervals, felt different in his chest — and then he had reached for a word he had already had, and something had taken him out of the room before he could use it.

---

She wrote in the visit log:

*Subject demonstrated expected sensory response during visit 7. Communication resumption: approximately 34 minutes. No distress noted.*

She had not been counting. She did not know where 34 had come from. She did not change it.

She picked up her bag. She said goodnight to the terminal. The screen showed nothing — he had gone again, or settled, or whatever the word was for what happened after.

She let herself out.

Outside, the rain. She walked home through it without adjusting her collar.

She did not sleep.
