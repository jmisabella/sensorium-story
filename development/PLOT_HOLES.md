## Hole 1: Why Rats Don't Get Ash Lung
**Status: RESOLVED**

CDFS is caused by persistent chemical compounds that bind to a specific surfactant protein variant found in human (and broad primate/mammalian) lung tissue, triggering progressive fibrosis. Rats have a slightly different molecular variant of this protein. The binding mechanism does not work the same way in rat lung tissue. Same contaminated air; different biological response.

**Crucially: the rat immunity is part of what led the Institute to the transplant program.**
Scientists noticed that rats were thriving in the most heavily contaminated areas while humans died at increasing rates. The question *"why are the rats healthy?"* became a research priority. The protein variant answer led directly to the question of whether rat biology could support transplanted human minds. The immunity and the choice of rats as substrate are the same discovery.

This can appear in the story naturally — Harlan or any informed character can state it: *we noticed the rats. We always notice the rats. They don't die here. We wanted to know why.*

## Hole 2: Rat Lifespan
**Status: RESOLVED — Option A**

The Institute developed a long-lived program rat strain through 40 years of selective breeding before the transplant program went operational. Wild rats live 1-3 years partly due to predation, disease, starvation, and harsh conditions — not because their biology cannot support longer life. Lab rats in optimal conditions already live 3-5 years.

**How it works**: Rats reproduce every 3-4 months. In 40 years, the Institute ran through approximately 120-160 generations of selective breeding — the same principle used to breed dogs from wolves, or dairy cows for milk production. In each generation, they selected the longest-lived individuals as breeding stock. Combined with controlled conditions (optimal nutrition, no predation, no environmental stress), the result is program rats that live ~15-20 years.

No exotic technology required. This is patient, deliberate selective breeding over many generations under existential pressure. It is actually *consistent* with the diminished-technology setting — they couldn't do advanced genetic engineering, so they did the old-fashioned thing and had four decades to do it.

Program rats are not distributed freely. They are bred and maintained by the Institute and assigned to approved transplant candidates. This is part of why the transplant program operates at managed scale rather than open access.

## Hole 3: The Suppressed Researcher
**Status: RESOLVED — see CHARACTERS.md (Nadia revision)**

Nadia IS the researcher. She was a virologist and environmental toxicologist at the Institute ~12-15 years before the story's opening. Her preliminary research suggested a possible molecular inhibitor for CDFS — not a proven cure, but a promising trail. Harlan reviewed it, told her it was "premature," had her reassigned, and buried the data in a restricted archive.

She grew suspicious — the reassignment felt wrong, and she discovered she'd lost access to her own data. When explanations didn't hold up, she left the Institute. She built the resistance network around the conviction that Harlan was hiding something significant. She has been right for twelve years but has never had the actual data to prove it.

**Narrative function**: When Maren finds the buried files, Nadia can recognize her own work before reading it — she designed the study. Maren has the evidence; Nadia has the context. Neither could act alone. Together, they hold the full picture.

**Key scene**: Nadia and Maren looking at the same data from opposite ends of a twelve-year gap. Nadia sees confirmation. Maren sees something more ambiguous. Neither — and therefore the reader — knows for certain whether the research would have worked.

## Hole 4: Timeline — When Did Ash Lung Begin?
**Status: RESOLVED**

The wars spanned roughly 150 years total, but the specific contamination that causes CDFS came from the *final phase* — the worst period of chemical/biological weapons use, approximately 80-100 years before the story's present. Not 200 years ago. "200 years" refers to the total conflict period; the relevant contamination is more recent.

**Corrected timeline:**
- ~200 years ago: Wars begin
- ~80-100 years ago: Final, most destructive phase; worst chemical/biological agents released into the environment
- ~30-50 years ago: Ash lung epidemic recognized as a population-level crisis
- ~40 years ago: Institute founded in direct response to the emerging epidemic
- ~11 years ago: First successful mind-to-rat transfer
- ~7 years ago: Transplant program operating at scale
- Present: Story begins

This timeline is biologically plausible for the contamination to have bioaccumulated across human generations and now be causing epidemic-level disease.

## Hole 5: What Happens to the Human Body Post-Transfer
**Status: RESOLVED**

The transfer process requires significant neurological disruption — the pattern is extracted as the biological substrate is depatterned. The human brain does not survive the process. The result is a clinically brain-dead body that ceases autonomic function within minutes. Bodies are cremated (burial in contaminated soil is not practiced in the city core).

**Why this matters for Nadia's argument**: the original person's brain is destroyed. Whatever continues in the rat is a pattern copied from a brain that no longer exists. The rat-mind's claim to continuity is internally consistent — it has the memories, the voice, the name — but the biological original is definitionally gone. The question *"is that Eli, or a pattern that believes it is Eli?"* cannot be answered from outside. It may not be answerable from inside either.

This should be stated clearly somewhere in the story — perhaps in Maren's understanding of the procedure, or in Nadia's argument.

## Hole 6: Why Can't Humans Filter or Relocate
**Status: RESOLVED**

Multiple overlapping answers that together close the door:
1. **Contamination depth**: 80-100 years of environmental saturation — soil, water table, building materials, atmospheric deposit. Not decontaminatable with available technology.
2. **Scale impossibility**: Clean-room environments exist for the Institute's research. Scaling to house tens of thousands of people exceeds the city's energy, materials, and infrastructure capacity.
3. **Incomplete protection**: Bioaccumulation happens even with partial filtration — food grown in contaminated soil, brief unfiltered exposures. Filtration slows progression in some cases; it cannot prevent ash lung over a lifetime.
4. **No better location**: The outer districts are more contaminated (former industrial/military zones). The core is the best available environment. It is not safe.
5. **Other cities are gone**: Expeditions to nearby cities found empty streets and abandonment. The city is operating in the knowledge that they are likely the last. Moving elsewhere means moving to somewhere that already lost.

## Hole 7: How the Communication Device Works — and Why It Degrades
**Status: RESOLVED**

**How it works**: The device reads electrical patterns in the integration lattice (the neural interface implanted during transfer) and filters specifically for human-language-associated pattern signatures. In early weeks post-transfer, the human neural signature is dominant. The device translates it faithfully — full sentences, normal vocabulary, recognizably the person.

**Why it degrades — two mechanisms:**
1. **Signal-to-noise shift**: Rat neural activity (sensory processing, instinctual response, spatial mapping) occurs continuously in the same lattice. As the rat mind asserts itself, rat-pattern activity increases relative to human-pattern activity. The device's filtering becomes less accurate — it produces shorter, more fragmented output because it can only confidently translate what's clearly human, and that threshold is being crossed less frequently.
2. **Calibration drift**: The device is calibrated at transfer time to the person's specific neural signature. As the rat sensorium changes the neural architecture over months, the calibration drifts. Recalibration is possible but permanently locks in the current (more-rat-influenced) state as the new baseline. There is no going back to how the signal looked on transfer day.

The Institute calls this "progressive calibration drift" in its internal documentation. It is not presented prominently to people considering the transplant.

**The Drift connection**: the early transplants who have retreated to city infrastructure also communicate less. Partly because they're physically further from humans. Partly because the signal has degraded to the point where extended communication takes significant effort. The device going dark for longer and longer before returning with shorter and shorter sentences is the measurable arc of a person being lost.

## Hole 8: Global Isolation — Why No Outside Help
**Status: RESOLVED**

Communication collapsed in stages:
- Eastern cities: silent for 15+ years
- Northern European cities: last confirmed contact 6-8 years ago
- London: silent for 4 years (already in docs)
- Nearby cities (within traveling distance): expeditions sent over the years — some returned. Reports: empty streets, abandoned buildings, rodent populations. Whether those are feral animals or transplanted minds is unknown. No one brought communication devices to find out.
- The Council stopped organizing expeditions ~3 years ago. Cost and exposure risk exceeded any realistic benefit.

The city has no functional long-range communication infrastructure. Radio signals meet no response. The Council does not officially state that they are the last functional human community — too destabilizing. It is commonly understood.

**Plot use**: The "London silence" can be a minor reference in passing. The "abandoned nearby city with rats" could be a larger detail — perhaps someone in the story mentions an expedition years ago, the only surviving member returned and said: *rats everywhere. Not afraid of me. Watching me.*

## Hole 9: The "No Alternative" Argument
**Status: RESOLVED** (synthesis of above)

The transplant is the last door because every other door is closed:
- Can't leave: orbital debris field
- Can't cure: technology too diminished, the one promising research trail was buried
- Can't filter or relocate: contamination too pervasive for available resources, no safe destination
- Can't expect outside help: communications gone, other cities appear dead
- Can't use artificial substrates: catastrophic failure in experiments, abandoned
- Rats work: immune to the contamination (protein variant), neurologically compatible after 40 years of development

The transplant is not chosen because people lack imagination. It is chosen because every other imagination has been tried. Maren knows all of this. She has believed it for years. Her doubt is not about whether humanity should survive. It is about what exactly is being saved.
