
### WORKING TITLE

Sensorium

### INITIAL IDEA

In distant future on Earth, humanity has waged war with each other to the point of near collapse. The war had progressed into space and as a result there is too much clutter from destroyed artifacts floating around Earth's orbit. Many have perished. Many data systems were destroyed as a result of the wars. Much knowledge was permentantly lost when the data stores were destroyed, as humanity depended too heavily on artificial systems and didn't pass important knowledge down to subsequent generations by story telling and teaching. Humanity has lost the ability to leave the planet due to too much debris in the way. Many have tried and failed, perishing in the futile attempts.

There are still scientists, though less technologically advanced than in the before times. Humanity has now existed in this diminished state for 200 years. Now there is new threat. Although humanity has ceased the wars, too much was lost. There is now a great plague wiping out humanity with no cure in sight. The scientists that remain are working on this day and night.

Eventually there is a breakthrough. A well established group of scientists eventually finds a way to transplant the human mind into other things. They originally weighed the idea of transplanting to artificial devices such as computers and machines, but the known risk of destruction is too high, as discovered during the wars in which previous knowledge was lost, not to mention the fact that thus far, experiements at transplanting human minds to artificial devices, at least in this stage with diminished technology has led to horrible and tragic/disturbing results that required subjects to be terminated for ethical purposes. But now this group of scientists has found another way: rats. Rats are resilient creatures. They have now successfully transplanted the human mind to rat, and have discovered a way to communicate with these rats.

So the plot point is that humanity is actively transplanting minds to rats and attempting to move society, all of humanity to rats. This seems the only way for humanity, at least human conscience, to survive. However, there are holdouts. There is resistance. People are horrified at this idea.

A plot point is that rebel groups of humans are trying to sabotage the mind--to-rat transplants. They are trying to destroy this altogether as they see it as an abominiation.

The main character is somewhat of a detective, trying to uncover who is behind this.

This story can explore ideas of body horror, existential threats, and mystery.

Perhaps by the end of the story, the main character discovers there is in fact a cure for humanity, there is hope, but by this point too much of humanity has been transplanted to rats, and in particular, this character's partner is already a rat and she does not want to lose him. By the end of the story she must make a choice: reveal the cure, or transplant herself to a rat. TODO: need to better hash out this ending idea...

### ADDITIONAL IDEAS

Lean into the detective angle: the MC starts investigating sabotage because they’re pro-transplant and only slowly realizes the cure exists. That flips the Paradox protagonist arc.

Make the rat experience intimate and grotesque in ways machines never could—intimacy, childbirth, predation, the constant smell of the world.

The final choice lands harder if the partner-rat still has fragments of human memory and is begging the MC not to join them (“I don’t want you to become this”).

### HOW TO TIE THE TITLE SENSORIUM INTO THE PLOT

The human mind, accustomed to a rich, evolved primate sensorium, is suddenly compressed into the alien, hyper-specific sensorium of a rat:
- Ultrasonic shrieks instead of speech
- A world dominated by scent trails and vibrissae (whisker) touch
- Constant near-infrared sensitivity, twitchy vibrissal input, frantic heartbeat rhythms
- The overwhelming flood of pheromones, hunger pangs, predator fear, nesting urges
- The grotesque intimacy of grooming, coprophagy, burrowing darkness

Every scene of a transplanted mind can be framed as the violent collision/recalibration of two sensoriums. The lead scientist/antagonist can use the word philosophically:

"The sensorium is malleable. We are not bound to one set of inputs. The pattern adapts; the sensorium is merely the interface."

The MC starts by finding this cold, dismissive: reducing a partner's entire lived experience to "interface." But as the story progresses, the word haunts her—every time her partner-rat twitches whiskers or freezes at a distant cat scent, the MC feels the brutal truth: the sensorium has changed, and something irreplaceable is lost even if the mind persists.

The scientist's or antagonist's line is perfect as written. "The sensorium is merely the interface" is chilling precisely because it's not wrong. It's the kind of line that sounds reasonable in a lecture hall and monstrous at a bedside. Keep it close to verbatim.

The coprophagy detail is brave and you should keep it. That's the moment readers will flinch and know this story isn't flinching either. The grotesque intimacy you listed — grooming, burrowing, the pheromone flood — that's where Sensorium earns its body horror credentials. Adrian Tchaikovsky did something similar with spider cognition in Children of Time and it's the thing readers remember most.

The MC's arc with the word is exactly right. She hears "sensorium" as clinical dismissal early on. By the end it's an open wound. That's how you make a title resonate — it means something different on page one than it does on the last page.

We need to have a scene where MC's partner-rat, mid-conversation, suddenly freezes and orients toward something she can't perceive at all — a scent, an ultrasonic frequency — and just leaves. Follows the rat impulse mid-sentence. That moment of the human mind losing to the rat sensorium in real time might be your most devastating scene.
